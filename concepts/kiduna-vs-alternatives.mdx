## What this page covers

- Side-by-side comparison with platforms, DAOs, communities, and traditional nonprofits
- Where each adjacent form falls short for the agentic era
- When a Kiduna is the right form and when it isn't

## Outline

### Kiduna vs. platform
Platforms provide leverage without governance. Kidunas provide governance and leverage together, bounded by accountability.

### Kiduna vs. DAO
DAOs offered self-sovereignty but often lacked legal standing — recent rulings (Sarcuni v. bZx, Samuels v. Lido) exposed DAO members to general-partner liability. A Kiduna is a DUNA, which is a recognized legal entity.

### Kiduna vs. community
Communities have meaning but no collective economics, no cooperative governance, no coordinated action, and no legal standing. A Kiduna has all four.

### Kiduna vs. traditional nonprofit
Traditional nonprofits have legal standing but typically lack member-governed machine-readable rules, on-chain treasury, and agentic infrastructure.

### When a Kiduna is not the right form
Closed enterprises, single-founder ventures with no cooperative dimension, ad hoc groups that don't need a treasury.
