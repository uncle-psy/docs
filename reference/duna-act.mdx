## What this page covers

- The statute and its effective date
- What a DUNA is and what it can do
- The 100-Member floor and other formation requirements
- What "governing principles" the statute recognizes
- How liability is allocated under the Act
- Where the Act is silent and other laws still apply

## Outline

### The statute
West Virginia Code §31D-1-101 et seq. Article 13 of Chapter 36. Effective July 1, 2026.

### DUNA defined
An unincorporated nonprofit association with at least 100 members joined by mutual consent for a common nonprofit purpose, elected to be formed under the Act, not formed under any other organizational law.

### Capacities
Hold property. Maintain a treasury. Enter contracts. Sue and be sued. Govern its members. Appear in proceedings. Configure agents.

### Governing principles
The Act recognizes agreements, consensus algorithms, smart contracts, and enacted policy proposals as the governing principles of a DUNA.

### Liability
The DUNA is a separate legal entity from its members for determining and enforcing rights, duties, and liabilities in contract and tort. Members are protected from being treated as liable merely because they are Members.

### Service of process
The Act requires a statement appointing an agent to receive service of process with the West Virginia Secretary of State.

### What the Act doesn't cover
Privacy law. Consumer protection. Securities law. AML / KYC where applicable. Employment law. Health regulation. Fiduciary obligations. Platform terms. Ordinary duties of care. Standing under the Act is necessary but not sufficient.
