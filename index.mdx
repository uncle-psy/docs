## The Kiduna Architecture for Collective Agency

A **Kiduna** is an organization with legal standing composed of humans and intelligent agents working together for a shared purpose. This documentation describes the conceptual architecture, the institutional design, and the technical stack that lets people join Kidunas, form Kidunas, sponsor Kidunas, and build Kidunas.

The name "Kiduna" is derived partially from the legal form it inherits, the West Virginia Decentralized Unincorporated Nonprofit Association (DUNA) — a member-governed institution organized around at least one hundred Members, a common purpose, and governing principles that can include blockchain programs, consensus algorithms, and policy proposals.

The original DUNA form was created to provide legal standing to Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs), an entity type owned and governed by its members, where decision-making and resource allocation are coordinated through blockchain technology rather than centralized systems and authorities.

In a DAO, members typically hold governance tokens that allow them to propose, vote on, and approve decisions. The organization's funds and operational rules are often controlled by blockchain programs known as "smart contracts" that automatically execute approved actions according to predefined rules.

The original promise of DAOs was to enable groups of people anywhere in the world to coordinate, govern shared resources, and pursue common goals without needing a traditional legal entity or centralized leadership. In practice, however, many DAOs discovered that while blockchains could coordinate voting and treasury management, they lacked clear legal standing, accountability, and the ability to enter into contracts, own property, hire employees, or appear in court — challenges that later motivated legal wrappers such as DUNAs.

The **"Ki"** in _Kiduna_ comes from **Kinship Intelligence Institute**, a 501(c)(3) public charity that can provide fiscal sponsorship and institutional support to Kidunas whose activities advance the Institute's public-benefit mission. Building on the legal foundation of the DUNA, a Kiduna extends the original DAO concept into an organizational form designed for the age of artificial intelligence. It enables humans and intelligent agents to participate as accountable actors within enduring relationships, economies, and communities, coordinating resources, governance, and action around a shared purpose.

Agency is the capacity to perceive, decide, and act in the world in pursuit of a goal. Throughout history, organizations have extended human agency through institutions, tools, and technology. Intelligent agents represent the next step in that progression. They can communicate, reason, negotiate, retrieve information, create content, coordinate work, and execute tasks on behalf of the people and organizations they represent.

Within a Kiduna, agents act as extensions of Members, Missions, Policies, and Alliances. Every meaningful action can be traced to a source of authority, a governing context, and a responsible party. In this sense, a Kiduna is not merely a collection of people or a collection of software. It is a framework for collective agency: a system through which humans and intelligent agents can work together to achieve outcomes that neither could accomplish alone.
